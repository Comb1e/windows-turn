The late-change stress case reaches about 646.35°/s display speed. Continuous derivatives do not mean an imperceptibly slow correction: the deadline can require comfort-limit exceedance. Simulated stationary input noise of up to 0.3° produces about 0.024° RMS displayed jitter. Supplied-velocity motion scenarios give approximately 0.01–0.02 s best-fit delay over their moving segments; this is not a hardware latency claim.

Trajectory tests match position, velocity, acceleration, and jerk on both sides of replanning boundaries; analytical derivative extrema bound sampled speeds/accelerations/jerks. Tests cover large source disagreement, stationary holds, reversal, model changes, and stale gaps. Missing readings retain the last display target for at most 500 ms without claiming a current measurement. Longer gaps freeze position but keep the deadline. Expiry is reported without granting another second. Stale-input freezing and final physical-boundary clamping are exceptions to derivative continuity. An unpredictable measurement arriving exactly at an expired deadline cannot be causally corrected earlier: `lastCorrection.completed=false` records such a missed deadline rather than hiding it with a jump.

The HTTP integration test runs the actual Fusion coordinator and Light Track service, with a deterministic keyboard service and synthetic pixels. It collects a sweep from 18° to a supplied 120° and back to 21°, launches the real Python Extra Trees trainer, checks Python/JavaScript parity, verifies immutable profile export and selection, then restores the profile in a new session. Synthetic profiles live in operating-system temporary directories and are not offered to the user as real calibrations.

Provisional sweep labels are explicitly keyboard, user-endpoint, or constant-speed-inferred. Raw keyboard values are preserved; unusable lighting samples are excluded from training while their keyboard labels remain recorded. Existing independent validation rejects inferred labels. Direction agreement cannot establish actual hidden-angle accuracy.
